NOTES:

Sigur Rós's sold-out show at the Heineken Music Hall sees me discovering that
I purchased not one, but two tickets in the fan pre-sale, all those months
ago. I have no-one to go with this evening, so I must now attempt to sell one
of my tickets.

Marktplaats is a non-starter. All of those scumbag 'secondary market'
agencies, together with scores of speculating individuals, are now scurrying
en masse to dump their extras for half the face value.

Last August's two Paradiso shows had sold out so quickly that I thought I'd
save myself the grief this time of having to pummel my keyboard with all the
other nutters on general sale day, and instead purchase my ticket as part of
the fan pre-sale. That turned out to be a bad idea.

Firstly, Topspin, who handled the pre-sale for the band, slapped ridiculous
fees on the tickets, to wit a EUR 5.85 per ticket handling fee and EUR 4.88 in
postage. When the tickets finally arrived, they had been sent to me in a plain
envelope from the UK, at a cost of less than EUR 1.

Fuck Topspin and their 21% mark-up. Fuck them very hard. Thank you.

Secondly, the gig sat on the concert calendar for months, not selling out.
When it did finally sell out, it was with just a week to go, but as mentioned
above, much of the demand was evidently artificial, with many tickets having
been snapped up early on by speculators.

I've used this platform in the past to rant against the secondary agencies and
individual ticket speculators, so I won't bore you with a repeat performance
now. Suffice it to say that I like them even less than I do Topspin.

Anyway, the net result of all of this is that I have to stand in the freezing
cold outside the HMH, looking for a passing punter to sell my extra ticket to.
However, it soon becomes clear that I am far from being the only vendor of
such wares, so I have to shift shop a few metres closer to the metro station
to accost passersby before they become spoilt for choice.

In the end, I manage to pass my extra ticket on to a nice Brazilian chap,
recouping its face value and losing only on the excessive Topspin fees.

With that out of the way, I waste no time getting inside the venue into the
warmth, setting up my gear and making my way to my preferred position for
recording.

The place is rapidly filling up. Everyone wants a good view of the action (if
one can realistically call the apparent inactivity at a Sigur Rós gig action)
and the floor is already quite densely packed, although most of the punters
currently have their buttocks parked on it.

I pass the time on WhatsApp, chatting to a taper friend of mine, who will be
manning the right PA stack this evening. A matrix of this gig may be in your
future if he determines that producing one would be worthwhile.

A couple of minutes before 20:00, Blanck Mass comes on stage. Yes, that's
'comes' conjugated in the 3rd person singular, because Blanck Mass is no more
and none other than Benjamin John Power, one half of curiously named,
Bristol-based electronic duo, Fuck Buttons.

Power's set is chilled out and beguiling. I knew I was going to like it,
because I had auditioned his album earlier in the day on Spotify. It's the
perfect entrée for what is to come, although there isn't much to look at,
because Power stands motionless at his keyboards and is, in any case, all but
obscured by Sigur Rós's all-consuming stage drape.

And then what is to come finally comes.

There is much excitement in the air. The band have announced that they will be
playing new material on this tour. In their own words:

  "Coming to a show is the only way you're going to experience this stuff
   outside crappy Youtube clips in the coming months."

Well, quite.

I had foregone the crappy YouTube clips, so tonight is to be my first
experience of the new songs.

I don't have to wait long for a tasty new morsel, either, because straight
away, 'Yfirborð' opens the proceedings, performed from behind the translucent
shroud.

That is quickly followed by last year's set-opening duo of 'Í Gær' and 'Ný
Batterí', after which the curtain finally drops to reveal the figures behind.

As I survey the stage, a growling bass and a plodding beat introduce the
second new song of the evening, 'Brennisteinn'.

I count eleven people on stage this evening, including brass and string
players. The brass section launch 'Brennisteinn' into a high orbit, from where
it slowly descends to Earth.

I recognise this sensation. A machine has been set in motion and there's no
stopping it now. The whole gamut of emotions will course through me tonight
like a swollen river. For two hours, I am but a vessel for the exhilaration
coaxed free by this band.

The music penetrates every pore of your body, every fibre of your being. It
touches you, reminds you of your humanity, stirs thoughts of your past, forces
you to ponder your present, wonder about the future. This music is akin to
staring into the smiling face of your children.

A grandiose 'Sæglópur' follows. It never fails to rouse me. I hang on every
note from start to rapturous finish.

There are so many highlights, too many to mention, but just listen to the
flute on 'Olsen Olsen', followed by the song's jubilant brass climax. Don't
you feel your pulse quicken?

The crispness of Georg Hólm's bass on 'E-Bow' comes ringing through with the
clarity of crystal. Jónsi's bowed guitar is drenched in so much reverb that it
sounds like a DC-10 taking off.

'Hoppípolla' is as joyous as ever. Ditto the pulsing, throbbing catharsis that
is 'Glósóli'.

The main set closes with our third new song of the evening, 'Kveikur'. Its
slowed down, chugging motorik beat is instantly likeable and, together with
the other new songs played tonight, it raises our hopes very high for the
band's next album.

The encore starts with Jónsi thanking us for being here this evening. This one
sentence will be the only time he speaks to us, other than via the universal
language of music. And really, words are superfluous tonight.

The familiar submarine sonar of 'Svefn-g-Englar', still the perfect vehicle
for Jónsi's plaintive falsetto, sets the encores in motion.

When 'Svefn-g-Englar' subsides to make way for 'Hrafntinna', we are onto our
fourth and final new song of the evening. It's an emotive piece with visceral,
satisfying percussion, featuring a vast array of tiny cymbals being struck.

There's really only one way to close the book on a Sigur Rós concert, and
that's with the song that reaches the parts that even the other potent
elements of this mighty band's canon cannot reach. That can mean only
'Popplagið', and then preferably in a fifteen minute rendition, which is what
we receive tonight.

The band's tradition of performing a curtain call is the final event of the
evening. Once the last of the eleven figures leaves the stage, the lights come
up and the spell is broken. The freezing cold night air and the bill for an
evening of severely overpriced parking await.

In a set comprising just sixteen songs, a quarter of them this evening were
brand new: the perfect mix of the old and familiar on the one hand, and the
new and exciting on the other.

My two recordings of the band from the Paradiso last year are amongst the
finest bootlegs I have ever produced. This one has a more spacious sound that
accurately reflects the much larger venue, but to my ears, it sounds every bit
as good. If you are a Sigur Rós fan, you are in for a real treat.

Samples are included to help you decide whether this is worth your while. Or
you can just take my word for it when I say that it is.
