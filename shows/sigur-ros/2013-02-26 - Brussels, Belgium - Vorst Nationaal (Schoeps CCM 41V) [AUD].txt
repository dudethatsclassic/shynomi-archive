NOTES:

This gig had started out as my insurance that I would get to see Sigur Rós at
least once on this tour. You see, the Amsterdam gig was due to take place
during a school holiday, and with those being our family's only opportunities
to travel, there was a significant chance that we wouldn't be in Amsterdam by
the time the gig rolled around. I thus purchased a ticket for Brussels, too,
in the fan pre-sale, musing that I would ultimately use one ticket and sell
the other.

Well, we ultimately didn't go away during the 'krokusvakantie' and so I had
the opportunity to see the band twice within seven days. I just needed to
drive to Brussels. Just that.

I've travelled to Brussels for gigs before, but usually not mid-week. As (bad)
luck would have it, I also had to hang around at home for some workmen to
arrive, so it was just before 16:00 before I got on the road, an hour later
than I had intended to be setting out.

What a nightmare. What should have been a 2¼ hour drive ends up taking close
to 3½. Adding in a pit-stop to answer the call of nature, this means I don't
arrive at the venue until 19:30. It takes a further ten minutes of driving
around the packed, narrow streets surrounding the venue before I spot a length
of kerb that looks just about large enough to squeeze my car into.

It works. I get out, gather my gear together and then leg it up the hill to
the venue.

Rushing through the doors of this completely unfamiliar place, I immediately
look for a sign indicating the location of the lavatories. I eventually find
the gents' on the far left-hand side of the building.

They want and extra 50 cents on top of the ticket price for the privilege of
pointing my penis at the porcelain. Oh well, the ones you have to pay for are
usually cleaner, I remind myself, because the sixty-a-day chain-smoking old
lady who takes your coin at the door has nothing else to do all day but mop up
the piss left on the floor.

I emerge from the cubicle as Taper Man, but now I can't find my way down to
the floor of the venue. It doesn't sound like a formidable intellectual
challenge, I know, but every door seems to lead to yet another section of
seats on the balcony. Aargh.

It's now getting perilously close to 20:00.

I eventually find the stairs, right in front of the window selling hamburgers.
Damn, I'd really like one of those hamburgers, or anything really, but there's
no time. 'Get the gig in the bag', I dutifully think to myself. 'You can
always eat later.' OK, it's only the support act, but I'm a bit religious
about my taping ('anal' is the word my wife would probably use). Besides, I
rather like Blanck Mass after seeing him perform in Amsterdam and I don't want
to miss his performance tonight.

I enter the main hall, but aargh again, it's one of those places where the PA
is mounted from a ceiling so high that it might as well be in the clouds.
There's no way to get near it for my usual kind of face-in-the-stack PA
recording.

The seats are all taken and, in any case, they're located so far back in the
venue that they're unappealing for my purpose, anyway.

There isn't much time and my options are limited. I move to the spot that
forms an equilateral triangle with those gigantic, suspended speakers on
either side of the stage. It's not my preferred location, particularly in such
a large venue (this place has a capacity of 7,000 and is sold-out), but it's
really the only viable option tonight.

Blanck Mass's set is largely ignored by the masses, or at least by the people
around me. There's so much talking and indifference that it might as well be
the barely audible music in the supermarket that you buy milk and potatoes to.

After the support finishes, I move forward by a couple of metres to get away
from the extreme chatterboxes around me and the lamppost of a geezer causing a
total eclipse of the stage in front of me. A few metres make all the
difference.

My new neighbour sees me changing batteries and realises I'm a taper. He
points to his microphones, currently dangling around his neck, and smiles. A
member of the same species! I've made a friend. He has driven down from
Rotterdam this evening with his girlfriend.

Just after nine o'clock, the lights go down and Sigur Rós come on.

What follows is basically a repeat performance of what took place in Amsterdam
five days earlier (and probably each of the days in-between, too).

The set-list has solidified, the only difference tonight being that 'Vaka' has
been brought forward and placed in front of 'Brennisteinn'. It's a subtle
change, subtle enough that I don't even notice it at the time. It's not until
I'm encoding the FLAC files for this recording that I notice that the set-list
for Amsterdam doesn't match what was played in Brussels.

I don't often find myself centre stage and, to be honest, it's a nice change.
From this angle, the band are much more visible behind the curtain that
shrouds them during the first new songs. The brass and string sections are
clearly visible, too, whereas they had been all but obscured from view in
Amsterdam.

With the entire stage in my field of vision, it's a treat for the eyes as well
as the ears. As a result, I probably enjoy this gig a little more than the one
in Amsterdam, although I couldn't say that one performance was superior to the
other. There is so little in it. The band play a blinder every single night. A
bad Sigur Rós gig? I don't think it exists.

It gets uncomfortably hot in the venue. It's freezing outside, but after a
couple of hours in here, the temperature has risen significantly. And that's
with a static crowd. I'd hate to be in here when people are dancing.

It's an epic finale again, 'Popplagið' exploding on stage in the way that only
'Popplagið' can. If the hairs on the back of your neck don't stand on end when
experiencing this song live, check yourself for a pulse.

As the lights come back up and I make my way out of the auditorium, I'm
praying the burger lady upstairs hasn't packed up and gone home for the night.
She hasn't.

I tuck into a cheap and cheerful belated dinner of 'braadworst', made more
interesting by the presence of ketchup and onions on the bun. It ain't much,
but after having been on the road all afternoon, with the stress of wondering
whether I was going to reach the venue on time, followed by more than three
hours of standing motionless on the same spot in stifling heat, it tastes like
ambrosia to me.

I follow up with a drink and then make my way through the fresh night air,
back to the car. At least it won't take as long to get out of Brussels as it
did to get in. Or will it?

They've closed all of the city's wacky tunnels for maintenance, so I'm forced
to drive above ground, incurring every traffic-light on the long haul out of
here. It seems as if the entire world is trying to get out of the Belgian
capital tonight. It's already after midnight, but it takes a full 45 minutes
before I finally hit the E19 and escape the gravity of this traffic black
hole.

I make it past Antwerp and up across the border near Breda. I'm somewhere
south of Lexmond when I finally hit the wall. I'm too tired to drive any
further, so I pull into a petrol-station, check my e-mail and then allow
myself to fall asleep. I wake up shivering 45 minutes later, start the engine
and continue on my way, finally rolling into the driveway at 03:15.

And then it's a 07:00 start to get the children ready for school. Lovely.

What has this got to do with music? you might be wondering. Absolutely
nothing, you're dead right, but hey, you're downloading this for free and it's
my prerogative to let you know what it took for me to be able to bring this to
you.

But now to what really matters...

With hundreds, if not thousands of people in front of me, rather than the
usual few dozen, there is necessarily more crowd noise on this recording than
on the one I made in Amsterdam. Being much further from the PA also lends this
recording a more spacious sound, which some listeners may prefer.

For my money, the recording doesn't quite beat Amsterdam. The music sounds
every bit as great, but the rapturous audience response that greets the songs
occasionally drowns out one of the more delicate beginnings or endings.

The Belgian audience also had the annoying habit of occasionally trying to
clap along, revealing themselves strangely unable to keep time. Nevertheless,
the audience participation adds extra drama to the climax of 'Popplagið' and
never forms a distraction at any point during the listening experience.

I will say, however, how tiresome it is that there are always a few people in
any concert hall who don't understand that when one of Sigur Rós's songs
softens into a whisper, it doesn't necessarily mean that it is two seconds
away from ending. Case in point: 'Svefn-g-Englar'. Don't whistle and don't
applaud until the music actually stops.

Anyway, don't be lulled into thinking that this is anything other than an
excellent recording. It sounds superb to my ears and I'm very pleased with the
result.

It's also good to have a recording that better captures the stereo image of
the performance and that does so whilst including more of the venue's
acoustics. Otherwise, what we'd have here would be more or less a carbon copy
of Amsterdam.

Samples are included to help you decide whether this is worth your while.
