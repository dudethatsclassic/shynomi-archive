Review:
The last time Sigur Rós came to Australia, in 2022, I had to miss out because I'd already spent
enough money on big arena shows around that time. But if you'd told me then that they were going to
come back and play with an entire orchestra on stage around them, I would have sacrificed seeing
their regular lineup in a heartbeat. Having heard about their various orchestral performances in
2023 and 2024, I was very much hoping it would end up making its way to Australian shores, and it
was an instant buy when the tickets went on sale last year.

I arrived at the arts centre early and had an anxious wait to be let in to my seat (which was
directly in line with the middle of the stage - kudos, past Claire), hoping that the MSO security
would be lax enough for me to tape (they had many signs everywhere saying "no photography or
recording", but once I was in my seat in the dark they couldn't really do anything about it). The
orchestra came on a couple of minutes late, tuned up, and were then accompanied by the Icelandic
quartet at centre stage (except for the drummer at the back, who has a seemingly very easy job at
these shows) to rapturous applause. The performance sounded great right from the beginning, it was
clear that they've gotten their practice in and sound great at every show now. It took a little bit
of time to adjust to Jónsi's vocals being accompanied by such a dense spectrum of sound, but we
were pretty golden beyond then.

They played two sets, nine songs each, with seven of their eight-ish albums represented in the
setlist (sorry, Kveikur fans, this show isn't allowed to scare people - I wish it was). It was a
well-paced show, too, with a strong sense of flow between the hits from older records and the more
fitting new material. 'Starálfur' was my highlight of the first set, naturally, but the second was
the better one - opening with two songs from the untitled album and closing with 'Sé lest' (the
marching band horn section was a delight to hear live) into a triumphant 'Hoppípolla' (which was
the loudest song of the show by far, and one where the conductor was practically leaping off his
stand).

I hadn't learned my lesson from the Eiko Ishibashi show, and so I had to boost the recording volume
up quite a bit in post-production after reducing all of the claps - it still sounds pretty good,
except perhaps at the start of 'Avalon', which the orchestra started while we were clapping the
main band off. Hopefully you enjoy this one!
