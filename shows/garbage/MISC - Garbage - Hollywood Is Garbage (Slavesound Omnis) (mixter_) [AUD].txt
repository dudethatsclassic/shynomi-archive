The Band:

The band makes it's 4th appearance in the city of angels over the past six months. The first three club dates were pre-tour "warm-up" gigs 
in which the tickets seemed to disappear faster than the band expected. After the ticket fiasco for their first appearance at L.A.'s iconic 
El Rey Theatre in which the scalpers got the lion's share of tickets and the diehards got locked out, the band added a second show the following 
night. They next added another "rehearsal" gig at L.A.'s Bootleg Theatre. The band made considerable efforts all along to accomodate their fans 
while continuing to underestimate their draw. Even when they finally scheduled their proper tour stop at L.A's historic Wiltern Theatre the show 
sold out in less than 72 hours. The band decided to change the venue to The Palladium to make room for another 1500+ fans. 

Ex-Jane's bassist-extraordinaire provided some serious Ridley Scott chest-bursting bass, a worthy addition to this tour.  



The Performance and Recording:

A respectable pull from the front half of the venue. Due to the fact that the front stacks are nearly 75' apart, the venue has a very peculiar 
center stack that is ceiling mounted above the front portion of the audience. I was positioned beneath and in front of the speaker cluster. 
Sound is direct but still has some stereo separation. Shirley's vocals are clear and sit nicely in a fairly dense mix of Erikson and Marker's 
angular and buzzsaw guitars, Avery's face-melting bass and Vig's Bonhamesque pounding. I would not expect the show to sound anything less 
than near perfect from this bunch of uber-producers and sound engineers. Although Avery's cabinet apparently goes to "11"! I not only had to 
roll off the bass @ 100 Hz in Audition but I had to reduce some pretty severe resonance that would occur intermittently most prominently during 
"Shut Your Mouth" and "You Look So Fine". Hence, there's not much below 28Hz here. The bass tends to rumble a bit hence the VG. The band played a 
flawless 2 hour set for a very appreciative L.A. crowd. This was evidenced by the pitch perfect audience choruses and fact that the band played 3 
new songs during their four song encore. A bold move by any band's standards.
 
Just a quick note... Less Chat = More garbage! Shirley looked amazing in her tartan dress and sounded equally as good. She gets a bit emotional 
while addressing the audience about the ill's of the record industry and how music changed her life.

I added my live recording of their Jimmy Kimmel Live performance the following night as a bonus. Pardon the editing between "control" takes 
and the other performances. The sound engineers would play pre-recorded music between takes. I parsed this as carefully as possible. There 
are no elements of the performance missing. Some of Jimmy's comments are present as well as those of his warm-up guy who bears more than 
a passing resemblance to a young Don Rickles (Mr. Potato Head from "Toy Story" for you kids out there). Oddly enough his name is Don as well.


Of note:

Shirley sings the final verses of "You Look So Fine" from the wings after having left the stage. Perhaps it was too hard for her to say a tearful 
goodnight to the audience after such a heartfelt performance.
