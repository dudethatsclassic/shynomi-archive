Review: Counting Crows, Royal Concert Hall
Monday, June 01, 2009, 11:07Comment on this story

MOST bands save the best until last at a gig.
But when Counting Crows' frontman Adam Duritz launched into an acoustic performance to start the long awaited and twice postponed Nottingham show, it promised to be very special indeed.
As Duritz sat on the monitors to watch other members of the band play the accordion and the banjo, or laid across the stage as he sang, he made you feel as if you were sat in his living room, watching the seven-strong band from California throw about new ideas.
The most striking part of the show was the vulnerability oozing from Duritz as he explained the reason behind every song.
Moving from When I Dream of Michelangelo, written at a time when his life was changing, Duritz talked about losing someone he'd loved because all he could focus on music.
 He described the ideas behind one of the band's best known hits, Mr Jones, written about dreams and the realisation that one dream coming true won't change your life.
Rain King earned the band a standing ovation and as they returned to do an encore -- a Beatles number -- every member of the audience took to their feet to dance along with them.
CLAIRE CARTER
