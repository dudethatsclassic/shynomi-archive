Foreword:

In the 15 years since its debut in 1993, Counting Crows first studio effort, “August and Everything After” has 
become an album that defined a generation. In those past 15 years, reviewers, critics and fans alike have gushed 
at the raw substance this Californian band were able to capture in the studio - there defined for all to see. It 
sold, and continues to sell, countless copies right across the globe. There was no doubting that this band of 
talented musicians had managed to deliver a remarkable record. To listen to this record alone, if nothing else, is
 to understand where and who Counting Crows were as at September 14, 1993. 

But like most bands who take an almost career long detour to “overnight success” there were other reasons for such 
a remarkable debut album. This band could play. And they had been playing for the best part of 4 years (as Counting 
Crows) and many more, incarnated as other bands, prior to this. Smokey clubs, darkened coffee houses and intimate 
venues were the breeding ground for the songs and the performances and the raw emotion that would eventually become 
the static version of AAEA. Crowds filled venues show after show, and by virtue of these wonderful performances, 
continued to do so for the next 15 years. Record companies sat up and took notice too, courting Counting Crows with 
record deals from far and wide. It was obvious that a complete picture of Counting Crows comprised, and always had, 
both their recorded works and their live performances.  

In the 15 years since coming to the world’s attention, Counting Crows have toured relentlessly. They are a touring 
band. A band willing to make a connection with their audience night after night. A band willing to give their audiences 
something individual to take away with them. A lyric. A movement. A guitar riff. From the US, Europe, Australia – audiences 
everywhere have been given something to treasure. Something so distinctive that only those at that particular concert, 
at that particular moment, on that particular night can understand it. Songs static at five minutes on record became 14 
minute improvised beings, electric became acoustic and acoustic became electric, melodies were lost and found and lost 
again and live shows went from breakneck speed to crawling patterns and back again. Songs became other songs too, with 
other artist’s lyrics ending up spliced in and around and under the Crows own songs, creating epic experiences for fans 
and sought after treasures for bootleg collectors. If AAEA was a static representation of whom and where Counting Crows 
were at that moment in time, then the live performances that followed represented how hard the heart of this band was 
beating from day to day. 

In an era where 11 songs became the initial representation of Counting Crows, countless others appeared live in concert. 
While not represented on this DVD, original songs such as “Margery Dreams of Horses”, “40 Years”, and “Open All Night” 
and covers such as “4 White Stallions” and “Wiseblood” were set list regulars – some of which still pop up in concerts 
today. 

“Waking Up Maria” represents the AAEA performances, in video, over the past 15 years. They are all live, loud, uninhibited 
representations of the songs as they were conceived, interpreted and played throughout the past 15 years. From the raw early 
performance of “Anna Begins” in England 1994 to the super slick, reinvented “Sullivan Street” of 2004, there is an honesty 
in these performances which is undeniable. A musicianship that is unmistakable. And a sound that bridges the gap between 
everything you thought you knew about Counting Crows when you first put them in your CD player, and everything you found 
out about them when you first saw them on your TV screen or in your town…..


DVD Notes:

DVD / Video trading has been an interesting phenomenon when it comes to Counting Crows. I personally consider that, until 
2003, “mainstream” trading of DVD performances was almost unheard of. Certainly, there was (and had been for some time) a 
small amount of people trading VHS cassettes amongst one another, but nothing on a huge scale. These factors were 
compounded by the reality that despite many promises, and many known recordings, Counting Crows were yet to (and at the 
time of writing this, STILL yet to) release an official concert footage DVD. Then, in 2002/2003, it exploded. The hugely 
popular “FogOnTheTyne” Live Collections (of which there are currently 13) surfaced, and perhaps with the increase in popularity 
or file sharing via bit torrent, and the advances in recording technology, DVD’s were suddenly popping up on boot lists 
everywhere. Counting Crows were also much more accessible on our TV screens too, especially in light of the “Shrek Effect” 
of 2004. These days, you can download a DVD from crowstown.com or hop over to youtube to see everything from 1994 SNL 
performances, to 2008 concerts from the current tour.  

“Waking Up Maria” is a DVD compilation of Counting Crows performances over the last 15 years. I have endeavored to not only 
find fantastic performances, but fantastic quality video captures. Some of these performances are already widely circulated, 
while some will have only been enjoyed already by a few. Some such as the Devore 1997 show, until recently, had not seen the 
light of day at all. 

Quality ranges from A quality VHS captures converted to DVD, to high definition digital broadcasts. There has been no compression 
used whatsoever in collating this DVD, and you can be assured that at the time of production, these are the best quality 
versions of these performances openly available. A 15 year time span obviously means that some performances were 
captured at a time when technology was not as advanced as it is today, while others have been broadcast or re-broadcast 
with a maximum of quality. I rate this entire DVD as A quality on both sound and picture. It is a collation of performances 
that over the years have been taped, collected, traded, seeded, swapped and circulated by many people – in both audio and video. 
While too many to mention by name, it would be ludicrous of me not to state the obvious fact that this DVD exists because of
all of them. Because of all of YOU. 

The performances are from a range of live gigs the Crows have done over the past 15 years, and follows the tracklisting from 
“August and Everything After”. TV spots, outdoor festivals, clubs shows, theatres, sheds and stadiums – it’s all here. Locations 
alter too, with everything from Chelsea Studio’s in New York to the open air stages of The Netherlands. And while the venues, 
dates and locations change, the intensity is always there to see. It is a DVD to further justify this band’s reputation. 

The DVD can be played as individual tracks or enjoyed as a whole concert. I have added a small track commentary for each 
performance, explaining what I feel to be the high points of the individual performance, and the history of the song in 
concert. None of the video on this DVD violates Counting Crows trading/taping policy, which outlaws audience video recording 
of concerts. 

Menu’s and chapters, additional processing and general DVD wizardry has been undertaken by Sullen.

Tracklisting Notes

01.
Round Here
2008.03.30 – A&E Private Sessions
A&E Television Networks
New York, NY, USA

Originally a song written by The Himalayans, “Round Here” became an instant mainstay of Counting Crows shows. Played acoustic or 
electric, “Round Here” is a Crows fan favourite. Early acoustic performances for radio show off a brutal intensity and musicianship, 
however it has been sprawling “alternate” or improvised versions that have become a trademark for Counting Crows. The band has 
often said that the recording of “Round Here” they laid down on “August” had never really been a true representation of the song 
itself, and countless live recordings of the song available are a vindication of that comment. Lengthy intro’s, alternate mid 
sections and outro’s became a trademark. 

I’ve chosen what I consider to be a brilliant version from 2008 to lead off this compilation. I’ve chosen this for a couple of 
reasons. Firstly, it’s one of the tightest versions musically I have ever seen. The band is true to the melody and the expressions 
are clear for everyone to see. Everyone is just “on”. While it may lack the sprawling organ introduction of the mid 90’s or an overly 
involved alternate, this is as “into it” as I have ever seen the band play this song on TV. Charley plays his “cyclone piano” segue 
to the bridge and from that point on, Adam pretty much loses his mind. The alternate goes from “did you think that you were dreaming” 
to the familiar “baby all I need is you” and then parts of “Have You Seen Me Lately?” There are some beautiful falsetto parts, and 
overall this is a truly pumped up, darkened, defiant “Round Here”. Being an early 2008 TV appearance after a long hiatus, “Round Here” 
at this time was all about Counting Crows showing the world they were back. This is “Round Here”. 


02. 
Omaha
1997.11.06 – Live at The 10 Spot
Hammerstein Ballroom, 
New York City, NY, USA

One of a few tracks to survive the early “Flying Demo’s” tapes. Originally a more electric song, the story goes that the band 
played and played this song in album rehearsals trying to find the right feel for it. During rehearsals, electric guitars became 
acoustics, big organs became piano accordions, drums became foot stomps and vocal melodies became harmonica’s – all in an effort 
to find out just how it should feel and sound. Once it was right, the impact was instant. In concert, “Omaha” has been a mainstay 
of the setlists, and a favourite crowd sing-along over the past 15 years. 

The “Omaha” I have chosen is largely representative of most you will see on video and was also released in audio on “Live Across A Wire”. 
It has a big accordion intro, crowd participation (although to a lesser degree of later shows) and showcases the bands versatility with 
a self styled upright, acoustic drum kit, thumping upright bass and plenty of mandolin. Charley rushes to centre stage for his moment in 
the sun with an accordion solo. The ending on vocals is certainly a high point. This performance was also prior to Immy joining as a regular, 
and therefore features Dan on Mandolin. From somewhere in middle America, this is “Omaha”. 


03. 
Mr Jones
2003.11.13 – Soundstage
WTTW Studios
Chicago, IL, USA

No song, for better or worse, has more widely represented Counting Crows to the broader community than “Mr Jones” – essentially the story of two 
boys sitting at a bar. The song has had many incarnations over the years, from the initial drum machine driven recording of the garage demo’s, 
to the clean electric recording on “August”, to the acoustic stripped down “Storytellers” arrangement and to the current “inverted chorus” 
arrangement of 2008 – it has been widely played and enjoyed throughout the years. Counting Crows have never felt obliged to play their biggest hit, 
but when they have, have played it as if for the first time each night. Over many years, through “Mr Jones” Adam has told us he not only wants to 
be (or doesn’t want to be) Bob Dylan, but also Alex Chilton, David Lowery, David Immergluck and others. 

“Mr Jones” from Soundstage 2003 kicks off with the trademark drum beat and guitar chords. I love this show because of how involved the whole band 
looks, and how enthusiastic the performance is. There are some things here you won’t hear on record, including Immy’s guitar solo into the bridge, 
and some subtle alternate lyrics “we all wanna be big stars, but we get so fucked up about that”. Here, we also see the lyrics towards the 
end sung as “I might be someone to believe, so come on believe in me..” – a departure from the original lyrics and perhaps the original thoughts. 
This is “Mr Jones”. 


04. 
Perfect Blue Buildings
1994.04.15 – Luxor Theatre
Cologne, Germany

At the Luxor Theatre in Germany in 1994, Counting Crows put on a show that would later go on to be known as “Adam the Untouchable” in bootlegging 
vernacular. That night, Counting Crows poured themselves out upon a stage barely big enough to contain them physically, in front of an audience 
less than 3 feet from them. “Perfect Blue Buildings” from that night is a perfect illustration of the intimate connection Counting Crows were 
managing to make with audiences right across the globe. It is a song steeped in imagery and descriptive phrase, outlining the tiny horrors life has 
to offer up. Personally, it is my favourite track off “August”. As a live track, Counting Crows often worked in part of Prince’s “Sometimes It Snows 
In April” in the outro – a definite bootleggers favourite. It has on occasion gone missing from setlists for periods of time.  

“Perfect Blue Buildings” from 1994.04.15 is everything I love about the song. It is raw, intense, bold unashamed and emotive. It has a slightly 
longer intro than usual with some subtle organ, and contains all the “light and shade” we have come to expect from his track. The crescendo point 
into the final chorus is just beautiful, and you can see the entire band feeding off one another. The bass, and drums (from former drummer Steve Bowman) 
on this track are particularly beautiful. This is “Perfect Blue Buildings”. 


05. 
Anna Begins 
1994.05.07 – Later…With Jools Holland
BBC Studio’s, London, UK

Perhaps no single female reference of Counting Crows lyrics has evoked more interest than “Anna”. For the record, a chance meeting with an Australian 
girl backpacking overseas was the catalyst for the song. A song about the right thing at the wrong time, “Anna Begins” has certainly remained popular 
among Crows fans and has been in and out of setlists for the past 15 years. It has been played originally in demo form, to being largely album 
representative, and also with extended intro’s (2003) and alternate intro lyrics (1994). At times, it is a much darker song that appears on record. 

In May 1994, the Crows played “Later” with Jools Holland in London, and produced a remarkable performance. A lengthy introduction and clear bass 
guitar with distinctive block beat drums wound their way around one another. Lyrically and melodically, this arrangement is relatively true to the 
recorded one, but has a much more bluesy feel, and builds to a magical crescendo of backing vocals, lyrical melody and crashing symbols. The piano 
through the choruses is particularly striking. Complete with tattered shorts, this is “Anna Begins”. 


06. 
Time and Time Again
1994.04.15 – Luxor Theatre
Cologne, Germany


The introduction guitar riff of “Time and Time Again” sends shivers up my spine. While played largely in its recorded format throughout the years, 
2006 saw the introduction of an extended, sometimes acoustic and sometimes electric, guitar intro, ending on a solitary resonating note before 
falling into the song proper. Early 1994 shows even had some alternate lyrics in the introduction. For some reason, this track is often listed 
as a Crows fans “least favourite” track along with some of the other darker cuts from August (Ghost Train also comes to mind). 

The “Time and Time Again” I have chosen is the only duplicate show (1994.04.15) on this DVD. Counting Crows played most of their debut album this 
night, and I felt this acoustic arrangement shows off a well styled and highly emotive recital. I love the backing vocals at different times through 
the song, and the growling lyrics sung by Adam. This is “Time and Time Again”. 


07.
Rain King
2003.02.04 – Heineken Music Hall
Amsterdam, Netherlands

“Henderson the Rain King” (a book by Saul Bellow) was the inspiration for this song, a musing of life, death and purpose. Another track to survive 
the early demo tapes, “Rain King” is as versatile as it is effective in its simplicity. Released as a single, “Rain King” is another audience favourite 
and set list main stay. Interestingly, it is one of only a couple of songs that Counting Crows have consistently improvised lyrics to throughout 
their entire career. Whether it was Springsteen’s “Thunder Road” or Gershwin’s “Someone To Watch Over Me” or Stephen Foster’s “Oh Susanna”, 
“Rain King” continually morphed into its own sense of being, it delivered its own heartbeat to the audience night after night, performance 
after performance. Versatile enough to play at large festivals (where shorter alternates were often used), radio shows (where acoustic, 3 
or 4 piece arrangements sufficed) or concert shows, “Rain King” always became a collectable moment. 

In 2003, Counting Crows played a 3 night run of shows at the Heineken Music Hall in Amsterdam and this performance is lifted from that run of shows. 
These shows were supposed to become an official Counting Crows DVD, but the project never saw conclusion. A full electric composition with some 
great mandolin, most stunningly, we have “Oh Susanna” as an alternate lyric sung through the mid section, with an entirely involved Amsterdam 
crowd singing along. It’s a live Counting Crows classic for a reason. This is “Rain King”. 


08. 
Sullivan Street
2004.07.16 – House of Blues
New Orleans, NO, USA

I consider “Sullivan Street” to be the “prettiest” song on “August”. In concert, it has developed from guitar driven acoustics to piano formed 
rolling melodies. In 2004, with the addition of a new drummer, I feel like this track finally reached its potential and grew into what it 
always could be: a living, breathing creature. While it is a song of a doomed relationship, I have always sensed a self respect in this song. 
It has been played during all the major tours however quite sparingly at times. My favourite “Sullivan Street” is probably from the 2004.11.16 
show and had there been a high quality video of that performance, I would have used it. 

Played rather solemnly early on in Counting Crows career, I have chosen the 2004 HOB “Sullivan Street” for the DVD. Here it is almost joyous 
in its delivery. The addition of “Runaway Girl” alternates here are amazing, and I love the instinctive drumming represented. Everyone in the 
band is just feeding off one another. Backing vocals and ad-libs through the middle of the song are sweet and succinct and I love the guitar work. 
This is Sullivan Street.  


09. 
Ghost Train
1997.08.12 – VH1 Storytellers
Chelsea Studio’s
New York, NY, USA

“Storytellers” is as iconic a performance as the Crows have ever given. Rearranged, unplugged and re-ordered, the Crows by their own admission 
decided to play an electric “Ghost Train” because they “really know how to play these electric songs…”.  A song about memories and living with 
the ghosts, or memories, of those now gone. “Ghost Train” is probably the “darkest” track on “August”.

“Ghost Train” from Storytellers has a great, dark electric introduction on guitar, and slowly builds through the intro. An intimate setting, you can 
feel the band connecting with its singer and the audience. The song rises and falls through the verses and chorus, and what stands out to me the 
most about this track, are the beautiful clean guitar breaks. The organ solo in the middle is beautiful and there is some fantastic driving 
drum work. This is a truly ambient song. Howling on the radio, this is “Ghost Train”.


10. 
Raining In Baltimore
1997.09.17 – Blockbuster Pavilion
Devore, CA, USA

Possibly the least played track from AAEA over the past 15 years, and certainly the least represented on video. “Raining in Baltimore” was even 
rarely played during the breakout tour of 1993/1994, and went virtually missing from set lists after 1997. As happens though, Counting Crows breathed 
life back into “Raining in Baltimore” by using it as an alternate spliced into the middle of “Round Here” and “Rain King” from around 2003 onwards. 
In 2008, it was played solo on occasion either as an introduction to or following songs such as “Love Song” and “Bottle it Up” by Sara Bareilles. 

The performance from Devore in 1997 is Adam solo on piano, accompanied by Charley on accordion. It is beautifully shot from a multi camera pro shot 
setup and provides an extremely intimate, piano based moment. There is a simple vocal melody and expression in Adams voice, and the subtle accordion 
which winds through the bridge is just beautiful. My favourite part of this performance is the end of the song, the built up howling expression of 
the lyrics making you think this would have been performed exactly the same way if there was no-one watching at all. This is “Raining in Baltimore”. 


11. 
A Murder of One
2003.06.08 – Pinkpop
Landgraaf, Netherlands

Along with “Round Here” and “Rain King”, “A Murder of One” is the most improvised song from “August” and has been an integral part of the majority 
of setlists over the past 15 years. Early versions extending beyond 15 minutes included a raft of improvised and substituted lyrics. Probably most 
well known is the “A Murder of One” from Paris in December 1994, the end of the “August” tour. High energy and fast paced, “A Murder of One” is a 
unique track on “August” and another crowd favourite. Often, names of towns were included in the final lyrics sung in the song, soliciting rousing 
applause and overall approval from crowds across the world. There are also “inside out” acoustic arrangements, simply with guitar, vocals and some 
basic percussion (a cardboard box on one early radio show). 

The recording captured from Pinkpop 2003 is wonderful for a lot of reasons. The bold, resonating organ introduction, the “step right up” exclamation, 
the subtle alternating lyrics, the huge crowd – we have it all here. The bridge section is especially tight too, building and building on the back 
of some great guitar work. What I particularly love is the ending. Everything from “Pinkpop tonight, Rotterdam on Tuesday” to Jim losing his 
drumsticks is just unbelievable. The guitar and drum work takes this song to a whole other level. I’d be surprised if anyone knew what was going 
on in the extended outro. “Go on Immy, get it up, go!” This is A Murder of One.
