NOTES:
Please do not sell this recording. 

This was the final Tool show before the COVID-19 shutdown. As I drove down to Portland from Seattle that day, the world as we knew it was changing drastically, rapidly. 
The WHO declared a pandemic that day, NBA games were cancelled, stocks plunged, schools were closing, and toilet paper was being hoarded. We still had very little idea 
what the virus was about to do to the world. If I had, I never would have gone to this show in a crowd of 15,000 unmasked people. But fortunately I did, and grabbed a good recording. 

There was a weighty sense of unease that night. But at the same time there was an oblivious, almost innocent excitement in the crowd and myself. Maybe the last time I felt it. 
The show itself was exactly what I was hoping for - a heaping spoonful of Fear Inoculum album. This is a meditative, sprawling and at times tedious album. Definitely not arena rock. 
It is fascinating to see them pull off presenting this material to 15,000 people, just as the progressive rock bands of the 1970s did back in that heyday. 

Danny was on tonight, lots of little improvs and generally just going for it. I think the band had just reached their stride with 7empest, after some shaky earlier performances. 
It was perfection tonight, and the tempo was ratcheted up. I don't even bother with the album version anymore; this performance is it. 

Great taping spot on this night - directly in line with the stack, Adam side, 17 rows back. The raw recording was close and full, but also had
challenging frequencies to deal with. Adam's guitar sounded gratingly tinny, Maynard was loud, and the frequency extremes dominated (bass and cymbals). I spent quite a bit of time 
smoothing all of this out in mastering, while still preserving the original sound as best I could. Enjoy!
