The Concert
Late 1979, Stevie Wonder embarked on a 6-city tour to perform his live rendition of
Journey Through The Secret Life of Plants -- complete with Orchestra.  Only six concerts
were ever performed of this entire material.  There are only two known recordings of any
of these shows (both made from this same Pasadena Civic show).  The other known recording
was made on better equipment than mine listed above.  Tragically the original taper of
the "other recording" is now deceased and his family does not allow access to any of his
recordings.  That makes this the only known recording that has ever been released to the
public of any of these shows.

During the first hour of the concert, Stevie plays the full "Secret Life of Plants" album,
 sans one or two songs.  After a break, he comes out dressed like "Little Stevie Wonder"
and plays 30 of his top-10 hits for the next two hours.  There's some very notable songs in
this list.  Stevie plays a few unreleased songs like "Garden of Love" and "Never Let You
Down."  He also does a great cover of "I Can See the Sun in Late December."  But the most
intersting song is an unreleased version of "Rock Like You."  This is the same exact song,
 albeit with different lyrics, as "Do Like You" that would appear on his next (Hotter
Than July) album.  As you listen to the song, note the totally different lyrics.

Notes:
This is the story of the long and twisted road to getting this show released to the public.

When this concert was originally released in 2007, it was done so in haste.  I had prepared a
set of CD's for my own listening pleasure early in 2000 and used the best available mastering
techniques at the time.  I always wanted to re-master the show in high definition, 96Khz, 24-bit
resolution, but I couldn't (as I'll explain below).  That's why it's taken another four years
since I first made this show available to the public.  But to explain that story, I have to go
 back many years.

In 1986, the master tape was stolen.  Until early 2000, I had no access to any copies of the show.
A long-time friend of mine came through with a copy of the show that I had made for his friend
before my master tape was stolen.  So in early 2000, I imported the show to my computer and used
the best tools available at the time to master it.  The only hardware available at the time
could sample the recording at 48Khz, or 44.1Khz. That is low resolution by today's standards.
The only noise reduction software available had the nasty side-effect of cutting bands of high
frequencies away from the recording as a means to reduce tape hiss.  The software essentially
destroyed the recording in order to remove tape hiss.

In 2007 when I originally released this concert, I no longer had access to the copy of my
recording.  I had no way to contact the guy, and no way to borrow the recording a second time.
However by 2010 things began to look up.  I was given the guy's phone number, but as a business
owner he had no time to rummage through boxes of tapes for me.  So while that sounds like it
was a dead end, I managed to track down another good friend who was missing in action for
the past 25 years.  I hit the jackpot with him.  He had copies of 10 different concerts of
mine, including this Stevie Wonder recording.  So late 2010, I began to work on it a second
time.  However as fate would have it, 45 minutes of his version was in MONO.  This forced to
call the other guy up and basically beg for him to look for the recording.  He came through,
so I now have two different copies to work from.

Today's [REMASTERED] version is different in every possible way than the original 2007 release.
In 2007, it was only 44.1Khz, 16-bit.  Today's version is sampled at an ultra-high resolution
5.6Mhz, 1-bit Double-DSD (near equivalent to 192Khz, 24-bit).  Noise reduction technology has
progress to perfection with the advent of iZotope RX-Advanced (I'm using RX2-Advanced).  Time/speed
correction is provided by Time Factory:  6 significant digits of precision for speeding up and
slowing down recordings.  And new to my arsenal for the first time:  pitch correction.  Pitch
detection is done by Melodyne Studio software by Celemony.
