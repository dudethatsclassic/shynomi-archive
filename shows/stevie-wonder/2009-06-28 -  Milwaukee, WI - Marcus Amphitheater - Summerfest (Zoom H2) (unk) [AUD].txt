An amazing, rare show for any R&B fan. The two have performed together at the BET 2005 Awards, and John Legend joined Stevie at one of Stevie's annual House Full Of Toys shows in Los Angeles, but this is their first time teaming up for a show as far as I know.

I was a bit worried Stevie may not show up. Michael Jackson died three days earlier, and Stevie didn't issue a statement because, according to his publicist, he was distraught. John Legend was a big enough name to carry the show on his own as well, so with that crutch, I figured Stevie may have taken the day off. Plus, the BET awards were on the same night as this concert, and Stevie is a staple of the network. I figured he may ditch his gig for a tribute or something. He did perform a concert on Friday the 26 in Kansas as scheduled, so my fears eased up a bit. Luckily, the show went on as planned. And it was pretty damn good.

The MJ tracks (other than I Can't Help It and We Are The World) are not performances, rather Stevie just celebrating and mourning MJ on stage. The ending medley or Jackson songs was the band dancing, singing and celebrating with the crowd. I figured I'd leave them in as Jackson's other boots are on here which are a good portion of playback.

This is my first recording, recorded on the low-cost (but certainly capable and efficient) Samson Zoom H2. I was center-left about ten rows back from the stage. I was closer to the left speaker stack that hangs from the rafters. I thought there was no way in hell I'd get anything other than a distorted mess. I was wrong. I am amazed at the quality that this low-cost recorder gave me. The sound at the Marcus Amphitheater, quite frankly, sucks. I can enjoy the concert better with these files than I could live, at least as far as clairty goes. The volume is low, and normalizing didn't do a lot of help since there was very slight clipping (A single split-second of clipping over an entire recording will prevent effective normalizing). However, you can easily fix this on your own, I'd rather just let people adjust things on their own.

But the show was amazing. John Legend had a  really good one-hour opening, and Stevie tore it up next. It was a long show... about four hours, over 3 hours after waiting and the intermission. And it was very emotional.

Rest In Peace Michael Jackson, gone but never forgotten.
