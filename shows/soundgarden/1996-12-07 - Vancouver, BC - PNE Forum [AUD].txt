24 bit 96 khz master clone split files > Wavelab toolsone (level boost and balance) > Waves Q10 Paragraphic EQ (bass ramp and mid-high tweaks) > Wavelab peak master (limiter @ -.01db) > 24 bit 96 khz batch rendered Remaster files > SoX resampler via Foobar2000 to 44.1 khz (very high quality setting, 95.2% passband, 25% phase response no aliasing) > iZotope RX Advanced MBit+ dither to 16 bit (Ultra noise shaping, high dither) > FLAC8 + torrent via TLH > YOU!!!

Quality:
The P.N.E Forum building is horrible for room acoustics. I've never had very good results out of this building, really. Having said that, I know there are Soundgarden recordings that circulate that are far worse than this one, and they're loved by the fans! I guess my recording isn't too bad after all, really! It ain't Adam Kasper's 24 track 2 inch analog master tape from the mobile truck, but it is far more honest than the protooling that I think was done to those tapes for the recent "Live on I-5" album from this tour.


Audioarchivist Notes:
It was my 29th birthday two days before this show, so it was a birthday present to go to this show. It was honestly a little disappointing to me, because I had recorded Soundgarden back at Lollapalooza 92 in Vancouver which was a great experience for me. So, this show paled in comparison. Now, so many years later, my perspective has changed a bit on this show. Not exactly as bad as I remembered it, I guess! haha. I have memories of how hoarse Chris Cornell's vocals were, sure, but the thing I remember most was how let down I was by Ben Shepherd. I don't quite know what was going on for sure, but we do know the band went their own ways not that long after this run of shows was done. He played with his back to the audience (when he played at all), and when he did turn around, he was kicking photographers and spitting at the audience. I love the guy's playing when he plays, lurching around the stage, bouncing his bass off the floor, rocking the hell outta that thing. He didn't do much of anything at this show but brood and attack the crowd, I think he gave us the finger, too. Must've been a lot of turmoil in the inner workings of this band at this time. There's a good story about the spitting and the kicking and the bad vibes in the review link I gave above. It's so good to see they've put all that bullshit aside to re-form recently. I hope I get another chance to tape them again one day soon!

I do have to call a bit of bullshit on the official release of "Live on I-5". The song "Rusty Cage" on the album was recorded in Vancouver at this show, or so they claim. I think that perhaps the bed tracks are from the show but edited, protooled and possibly overdubbed a bit. Maybe not new recordings, but I'm sure a bum vocal or twenty have been replaced from the other show nights multitracks. Also, the guitar intro on my recording is chopped down to nothing. I was flipping tapes when CC was dedicating the song to "...the man in black..." as is on the album, but that extended guitar intro is there. Depending where you're downloading this, I may have had to cut Rusty Cage out to comply with some site rules. If so I will do my best to include as much pre and post song crowd as I can (with the missing guitar drone included) so the official track will fit in to place better, or you might luck out and grab this from somewhere else complete!

On my master after the show's over I am on tape talking to someone else I spotted in the crowd recording as well as me. I don't think their gear was quite as "advanced" as my basic Sony Pro WM D3 rig - I seem to remember something more like a Radio Shack micro cassettecorder, but they had the whole show, too! I try to swap telephone numbers and make a trade with the guy, but I think he misunderstood my intentions - not to bust him but to trade my tape of the show for his tape! He walks away and I'm left feeling weird and bad. Bummer. Point being - there is a second source for this show (besides Adam Kasper's 2" 24 track masters), but the second source is deep undercover I guess! Maybe this can be considered a plea for that recording to surface, too, now that mine is out there! 

I've had a myriad of requests for this show. I've been taping concerts since 1989, and have well over 200 shows on tape, minidisc, or memory card now. I've probably put out 1/4 of them so far. Transferring the old analog tapes is rather time consuming to do correctly. I tried a couple years ago to get this show out, but a failing tape deck at the time stopped me. Then, things got too busy and I couldn't do anything with it. I do intend to release it all, but as John Lennon said: "...Life is what happens to you while you're busy making other plans..." and, well, life keeps happening to me while I plan to put all my recordings out there...

One of those "Life" things recently has been that I've been gaining access to three other taper friends collections and beginning to transfer some of their tapes. There are a few "famous" tapers who have become well known for either having their collections recently transferred and posted, or they are known maybe for they're lack of releasing their masters. haha. My bigger plans are to bring my 3 buddies recordings to light. Each one has been very prolific in their own right, taping hundreds of shows each through the 70's, 80's and 90's too. They are all aware of the Lampinski tapes for example, and they all have similar quality recordings in the same huge numbers. They have all expressed interest in getting their stuff out before its all too late, and since I'm the guy with golden ears to work these shows, they want me to get them done. So, make room on your harddrives people, Fred's and Kevin's and Trevor's works will start to trickle down this summer. Plus, there's my own shows to deal with, too!

Please share this show freely, for free. Not a don't ask don't tell policy, but a don't buy don't sell policy! Share the whole fileset (or as close to a whole set as you can get - missing track or not) with the text files and checksums and pictures included, please. If you have to add your own notes, make a separate text file with your additions and include that too, but don't leave mine out! If you absolutely have to encrap these files to eMPty3 or other lossy perversions to play them on your iPood, then do it, but don't upload the lossy files to your blog so your friends can hear it. Friends don't let real friends listen lossy! Burn audio CDR's and throw them around to your buddies, but if you think they're going to share it forward, give them the full FLAC fileset, too. Please don't Re-Re-ReMaster this version of this show. The files have already been processed once, as well as them being "only" CD quality files. One day I plan to post the hi-res raw master clone files at 24 96 both for the audiophile bootleg purists and the other wannabe rebastardizers out there! hahaha. 

I've scanned my tapes tickets and cassette covers, plus some of the handbill flyers that were floating around town before the show. Those links I gave above point to a few pictures from the show, too. If you're a hipster graphic designer, or an amateur doodler with a cracked version of Photoshop, and you have a hankering to make some cool covers for this show, well, please do it! I just ask you to please post them in a comment so we all can share them, and if you could also email me a copy to make sure i get it, well, that'd be awesome! Remember, bigger hi-res art is better than shrunken blurry jpegs, so make them big!

Enjoy this "new" old master. Cheers!
