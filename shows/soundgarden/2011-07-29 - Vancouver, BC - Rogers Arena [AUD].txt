Notes:
This must be one of the top 5 loudest shows I've ever been to! I'd also have to say it's one of the top ten best sounding shows I've ever attended. I find it hard to believe that I can say these two things about the same show, but I was very impressed with how "loud and clear" this show really was, for the most part. There were several times that I thought Kim's lead guitar should have been about 3 times louder in the mix than it was. Maybe if everything else was turned down a notch it all would have balanced better, but it wouldn't have been a real Soundgarden concert without a large dose of hearing damage for all the concert goers!

This recording is the first of three that I will be releasing. I recorded the whole show from three completely different recorders and mics. I also used with a Zoom H2 with my Sony ECM DS-70P mic, and a Zoom H1 with a Sony ECM-907 mic. I ran this third cheezy little recorder "for fun" as a backup to the backup of the main recorder. It came out sort of alright, so I worked on it a bit to put it out first. For fun. The other two will come out soon, and, yes, of course they're much better than this one. I was just thinking that if this was all there was and I didn't have my good recorders working that it wouldn't be too bad. So, I thought I shouldn't just ignore this lo-fi version because even though it isn't exactly "great" it isn't that bad at all, and if I put the good ones out first this one would totally get ignored... This version comes from my "anything but an iPod" mp3 player that I bought from London Drugs for about $50 plus tax. It recorded a 16 bit 44.1 khz wav stereo file, but the single point mono voice recorder microphone clearly makes it a mono master recording. The Sansa Fuze by SanDisk isn't really made for recording much more than, well, I don't really know. Meetings? Lectures? Notes? I wouldn't normally say that it is for live music recording (unless it comes off the radio!)... I mean, it usually just doesn't cut it for heavy duty recording, but the RockBox operating system makes it seem like it's a better machine! Once I got the RockBox firmware on it I felt like I had a cool player. Drag and drop FLAC files, has a micro SDHC card slot to expand to 32gb, its a sweet little player! Still, it is a pretty lo-fi recorder, giving the recordings from it a vintage 70's dictaphone tape quality! hehe. I think that the great concert sound they had at the show comes through in this recording! So, don't ignore this crappy little recording! haha.


As far as what I did to this recording, after correcting the DC offset I EQ'd up the upper mids and lower highs, and sharply rolled off the extreme highs. There was interference in the very upper highs from internal data passing through the machine - a high squeaking pulsing that had to go away! Then I ran it trough some custom reverb to simulate a stereo field and I think it more or less matches the delay times that GM Place - I mean Rogers Arena actually has. I think that except for a few unnatural sounding loud localized claps between a few songs (I think it's me clapping! haha) the stereo picture is amazingly normal and real for an arena concert tape. It kind of feels like the simulated arena stereo reverberating reflected sounds from the sides aren't simulated at all... So, a lossless mono master stereo simulated remaster is what we have here. I think that its amazingly good for how kind of cruddy it really started out!

Try it out - it's free! Nothing to lose, really, right?
