Mastering details:
Both OcenAudio and Reaper use 64bit float internal representation for the audio data. The transfer between the programs is 32bit IEEE float PCM. Overkill for bootleg recordings? Sure, but I'm OK with overkill.

OcenAudio (destructive)
	convert WAV to IEEE float 32b CAF (this is for import to Reaper)
	adjust L/R to be roughly equal RMS and average power, normalize, and remove DC
	additional minor clap and transient fixups that appear after Eq mastering

Reaper v7.x (non-destructive)
	EQ and readjust for 0dB peaks (IK Multimedia TR5 Linear Phase EQ)
		additional peak limiter on difficult transients (Cockos ReaLimit)
	brickwall limiter (IK Multimedia TR5 Stealth Limiter)
	render to FLAC, songs/tracks to individual files

Media Monkey
	tagging and file renaming
	batch conversion from master format to lower res FLAC for anything below 96kHz24b (my native library format)

About the software I use:
OcenAudio is a fantastic and very stable (destructive) audio editor that runs on Mac, Windows, and Linux. I contribute to their project. I hope you will too.

Audacity very rarely, just for specific filters.

Cockos Reaper is a great and very reasonably priced DAW that runs on Mac, Windows, and Linux. It is the backbone of all my mastering work. I use the paid personal edition and love it. It has a bit of a learning curve, but it's worth the effort. If you need a DAW on the cheap please consider trying it out.

IK Multimedia various VST/AU plug-ins. I primarily use the Eq and limiting.

Media Monkey is a Windows only media library manager that I've used for many years now. I don't know what I would do without it.
