From the Berkeley campus, Jeff and band returned to the GAMH for a second, sold-out show where 500 or so lucky punters were treated to an extremely spirited performance. After eight shows in four days, the band is firing on all cylinders. The recording is a little over-saturated at points, but it's a solid listen. Unlike the previous evening, we are able to hear the entire show, uninterrupted.

The set doesn't stray too far from the usual suspects, but we are given a couple of treats: Jeff's solo spot begins with a rare outing of another song associated with Nina Simone, "The Other Woman" (my personal favourite of the handful of her covers Jeff assayed over the years), before continuing on to "Hallelujah" which on this occasion includes the Smiths interlude.  

After the band return - the encore break appears to be edited out - a fan calls out for "Morrissey" while another shouts, "Free Bird", to which Jeff responds, "The Free Bird Is Dead", and proceeds to improvise on the idea in Morrissey's voice. Then Jeff says:

	"We're gonna do something that we didn't do yesterday. It's a bit of an instrumental but someday this will be a [unintelligible] song and you're the first ones to hear it. So you don't mind, right? Someday this will be called, "The Sky Is A Landfill"". 

It's a far cry from what we were given on "Sketches", with tentative partial lyrics, and still unfinished - Jeff calls out some of the chord changes - but it's great to hear it in its nascent form. This is the only recording until 18 months later in Buffalo, by which time the song had changed quite a bit. Kanga-Roo follows to close the show, without its usual Vancouver intro.

I got this from BLG's 2014 Dime upload, and apart from some basic housekeeping, present it as-is. BLG's notes follow.
