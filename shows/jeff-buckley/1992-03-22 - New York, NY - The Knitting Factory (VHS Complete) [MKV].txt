This is the "complete" version of this very dark VHS transfer, missing the three tracks which were officially released in audio form on "Songs To No One" https://www.discogs.com/master/67549-Jeff-Buckley-Gary-Lucas-Songs-To-No-One-1991-1992. I received this on a trade DVDr 20-odd years ago. Since some of it has been officially released, I decided to offer it up in this expurgated for the historic value, at the very least. This is the longest extant Gods & Monsters show on film, and it's a unique snapshot into Jeff's earliest days as a performer. He had just recently cut off all his hair, and the partnership with Lucas was coming to an end. Nonetheless, you should get a good idea of what a Gods & Monster show was like.

This must've been a great evening of music at The Knitting Factory: Nick Hill announces the rest of the evening's music would include Peter Case and the best part of The Golden Palominos - Anton Fier's side band with Sid Straw, Peter Blegvad and others. You can catch a brief glimpse of Peter Case taking the stage with his guitar during the station ID.

As usual, Jeff is a cut-up on stage, in this instance offering teases of Nirvana, Tequila, and Led Zeppelin. The proper G&M set begins with one of only two known performances of "No One Must Find You Here" (http://www.flowersintime.org/show.php?songid=107) and "Distortion" (http://www.flowersintime.org/show.php?songid=33). Additionally two of Gary's songs are played. Both can be found on Songs To No One. Finally, we get the first full band versions of both Grace and Mojo Pin, both of which are still more Gary than Jeff, but getting there. The show concludes with a stunning six-minute reading of Edith Piaf's Hymn á l'Amour.

I got this on a trade DVDr 20-odd years ago. It's a bit on the dark side, but it's a solid watch. I hope you enjoy.
