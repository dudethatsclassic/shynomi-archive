Jeff played (and made cappuccinos and bussed tables and washed dishes) at Sin-é starting in May of 1992, and we have about two dozen documented appearances there, culminating in his New Year's Eve show in 1995/6. He did play (virtually) every Monday night between May and August of 1992, and returned many times after that. He talks about his time at Sin-é in the interview just posted as part of the Music Faucet broadcast from 11 October 1992.

lovesick alien's notes 2024

As the original uploader noted, these are NOT the recordings featured on the commercial release Live at Sin-é. Those recordings were made on two nights: 19 July and 17 August 1993, in front of an invited audience of industry professionals and Sony hangers-on, with the intention of a commercial release - first the EP and then, after Jeff's death, the CD/DVD edition.

Confirmation from Jeff that the commercial Sin-é release was for an invited audience of Sony personnel & friends: https://notwithyoubutofyou.blogspot.com/2018/01/the-child-prodigy.html

I have carefully A/B'd these versions against the commercially released tracks on Live at Sin-é and elsewhere, and I'm satisfied these are not commercially released. Here are the chief differences in the performances of the songs found in both places:

Hallelujah #1 - On Sin-é, entirely different opening guitar section
Hallelujah #2 - On Sin-é, entirely different opening guitar section
Funk 49 / The Way Young Lovers Do - on Sin'é, completely different performance with lengthy vocal intro and no Funk 49
The Twelfth of Never - on Sin-é, different performance; on this recording he dedicates the song "to someone who just walked in"
I shall be released - on Sin-é, a different intro & different vocals
Calling You - on Sin-é, Jeff says, "Haven't played this in a while" during the intro
Strange Fruit - on Sin-é, an entirely different guitar intro/vocal performances
Sweet Thing - on Sin-é, there is no talking during the intro (unlike this one) and a different vocal performance
Dink's Song - entirely different with scat vocals at start, and lacking the chat found o this one..
If You See Her, Say Hello - on Sin'é, Jeff plays slide guitar and comments upon being recorded
Eternal Life - on Sin-é, the intro is surer and shorter; here it is quite a long intro
Sweet Thing - on Sin-é, there is no talking during the intro and a different vocal performance

I am very grateful to the original taper for having preserved these magical moments from early in Jeff's career, immediately after his signing with Columbia, but pre-dating the July and August recordings which were professionally recorded and ultimately released by Sony. My only regret is that (s)he didn't identify the original recording date of each track, which would have made things a LOT easier! LOL

Interview/Review of the Sin-é EP here: https://notwithyoubutofyou.blogspot.com/2017/12/raygun-interview.html
