By way of introduction . . .

This is one of the most misattributed, mislabeled shows of Jeff's in circulation. I have spent a long time researching this one, and I'm now convinced of the actual nature of this recording. Let's start at the beginning, with the well-known story of the provenance of the tape. Sometime during the fall of 1996, Barry Paddock was walking down 2nd Avenue in NYC. At 12th Street he spotted a pile of cassette tapes left on top of a garbage dumpster. He played through the tapes to see if there was anything worth saving. Among the collection of pre-recorded albums was a blank tape labeled "J. Buckley Stuff". Barry saved that tape from the trash and took it home, where it was much duplicated and circulated amongst Buckley fans

Initially, this was mis-identified as a tape of the demos Jeff made on 17 January at 21st St. Studios in NYC. Per FIT, "Often confused with the WFMU show. As Dan Brown's book 'Dream Brother' states, he actually did a session in West 21st Street Studio, and he even mentions 2 of the tracks on this tape. . . . this track list is dated as 17th January WFMU Trashcan Tape, which has probably led to the confusion over the years. 17 January is also listed as a Music Faucet date on Flowers in Time. Dates attributed to the early days of Jeff's career are notoriously unreliable, but these are two of the best and most comprehensive sources, so I'm going with the January date. 

So far so good, right? Only not so much. Let's turn to Buckley biographer Jeff Apter. Per Apter's Buckley biography, this is most of the 17 January Music Faucet set:

"Although overlooked by most historians, the January 17th set with [Music Faucet host Nick] Hill was an especially significant event for Buckley bootleggers [sic]. He strummed and crooned his way through nine songs, all covers, including takes of [lists all the tracks on the 'Trashcan Tape']. . . . But what's most interesting about the set was the disappearance and chance discovery of a recording of the show [here follows a re-telling of the Barry Paddock story]." 
        -- from Chapter Six of "A Pure Drop: The Life of Jeff Buckley".

Based upon this evidence, what we have is an apparently incomplete recording of the 17.1.93 Music Faucet programme: sources (including Apter) say "The Way Young Lovers Do", "Je Ne Connais Pas Le Fin" & "Dink's Song" were also performed that night, but a complete version of this recording has yet to surface if that is indeed the case.
