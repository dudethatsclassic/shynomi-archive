Comments: 

Final U.S. show from Them Crooked Vultures' first U.S. tour -- a 
"hometown show," as Josh Fromme reminded the crowd several times 
as a nod to their debut performance at The Metro in August 2009. 
The emotion of the closing night may have led the band to play a 
slightly longer set than usual, and the rhythm section of John Paul
Jones and Dave Grohl was overpowering. I'm still not sure how 
music I like the music itself, but the drum/bass combo was 
mesmerizing -- the tightest combo I can remember seeing live. 
The crowd was very into it, but respectful -- practically adoring
-- of the band, particularly Jones (a dynamic I thought was 
particularly cool since 95% of the audience members around me were
not yet born the last time Zeppelin toured the states in 1977). 
The Aragon can be a tricky venue both for taping and for passive 
concert-viewing because the acoustics are so notoriously poor that
the audience sometimes loses focus ... for this show, the band got
100% of the crowd's attention for two solid hours without a break. 
I haven't listened to many shows from this band, but I have a
feeling Chicago got an unusually good show. 

Quality-wise, I was pleasantly surprised with the sound given how
tough it is to tape at the Aragon. The low end (drums/bass) was 
overwhelmingly strong from my spot on the floor (I was ok with 
that, given that JPJ and Grohl were who I paid to see) and the 
vocals and guitar are audible, though slightly down in the mix.
The typical muddiness that usually ruins most Aragon Ballroom 
recordings from this venue wasn't too bad and was largely 
mitigated with some light EQ reduction in the +1 kHz range. 
Audience chatter doesn't intrude too much during the show except
during between-song banter and a few quiet spots. It's far from
the best audience recording I've made, but definitely listenable. 

Jonohull posted a nice audience recording right after the show, 
and I initially figured I wouldn't bother to post my own. However,
when I compared the two, I found the dynamics of my master are
almost the opposite of his -- the other source has very clear
vocals and guitar, but I found it to be light on the mid-range
and low-end; my master has prominent low end and the vocals/guitar 
are much lower in the mix. I suppose a good engineer could matrix
these into a "definitive" version, but I thought I'd post mine
just to provide an alternative perspective on the show. Whichever
one you prefer is definitely a matter of personal taste -- several
mp3s are included in the comments to help you decide whether 
it's worth your while. 

Given the attention this band has generated, I thought some might
find it worthwhile to grab an alternate source. Hope that many 
of you find it worth the bandwidth.
