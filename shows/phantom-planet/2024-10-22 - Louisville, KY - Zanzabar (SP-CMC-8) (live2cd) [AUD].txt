Notes:

*  Denotes partial.

So, 24 hours prior to this show, I had absolutely no plans to be there. I had a
ticket for their show in Columbus and planned on recording that. However, other
forces intervened (in this case an older woman reporting seeing my cables coming
out of my pocket to security), who then came over and told me to take everything
I had back to my car. That was a first for me. The show hadn't even started yet.
Her son told me she thought I had a bomb. He later apologized for her. Security
told me I couldn't record audio with my SP mics because it was "illegal", however
they mentioned there was "no problem" if I held my phone in the air all night and
recorded that way. I really hate this ideology. I only want to capture good audio,
but they have no problem with a handheld device capturing 4K. Anyway, they made
me run my gear back to the car 5 minutes before they took the stage, so I ended
up missing the first 2 minutes of "The Happy Ending" and got the rest on my phone.
Feel free to look it up on YouTube. SO, because of this debacle where some woman
thought I had a bomb, I planned an impromptu trip to Louisville the next day to
record Phantom Planet performing their terribly underrated 2004 Self-Titled album
in its entirety. On the positives, the sound was much better in Zanzabar than it
was in the A&R Music Bar in Columbus - but on the negative side, the setlist was
20 minutes and 4 songs shorter. In Columbus, they performed the 2004 b-sides:
"The Living Dead" and "Stiffs", however, Alex decided to nix these at the set break
in Louisville. The show was very loose (maybe 70 people total?), so there was a
good amount of banter between the band and audience. At one point, the band asked
a fan in the front row to bring them some beers. While waiting, Alex made up
a song on the spot for Daniel (who was then at the bar). Pretty funny stuff. The
2nd set was mostly request driven, but it was weird not hearing "Lonely Day" 2
nights in a row (weird!?) and seeing them skip "Anthem" in Louisville. Those are
staples! But, I went for the ST, so I can't complain. I got what I came for and
this time no one thought I had a bomb in my pants. Crisis averted in Louisville.
