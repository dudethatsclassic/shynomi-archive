A friend of mine had this tape.  He was the owner of the D3 that
the show was recorded on.  A friend of his is the one who did the taping.

He had mentioned once in awhile over the years that he had an NIN tape, 
recently I had him dig it out and realized that the show was not commonly 
traded and not in the RITC NIN Archive.  I was at the show after recently becoming
introduced to NIN via the live broadcast of the Woodstock 25 performance.
I was so glad all these years later to find out that it had been recorded.

The quality of the recording is pretty decent.  The first two songs
were kind of blown out volume wise.  But it gets better.
The quiet parts of the show are very nice.  The taper had some seats 
that were labeled 'obstructed view'.  I'm guessing that some of the on stage
equipment and the P.A. was blocking the view of the performers.

At times you can hear that the main part of the audience seems to be in the 
right channel and the band more in the left.  So I'm guessing that the taper
was way over on one side.  Whenever I work on a show I try to put the audio
straight down the middle like the taper was in the sweet spot.  I've done
that somewhat here although it was kind of hard to completely disguise the tapers
location in the hall.

The supplied stereo microphone that came with the D3 is somewhat lacking in
capturing the low end.  The tapers location and the size of the arena also may
have contributed to the undefined low end. Tried to compensate for that.

I've been quite conservative here with the final level of the audio.
There's no brick-wall compression.  Turn it up!

The FLAC files are tagged.  If burning to CD split between track 8 and 9.
There was a tape flip there and I left the break intact for cd burning.

I'm also including some screen captures that show before and after what the waveform
of the whole show looked like and before and after FFT views of the whole show.
