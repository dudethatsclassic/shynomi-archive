First, I'd like to congratulate whoever took two mono audience recordings and made a stereo
matrix from them. That must have been *a lot* of work, given that the speeds of any two of the
mono portable analogue tape decks of that era seldom if ever matched up exactly.  The amount of
editing between the two recordings which needed to be done to make a complete show must have
been a Herculean task as well. It's rather well, trippy to hear a stereo perspective of this show,
even if the stereo image kind of jumps around on you. Contributes to the "psychedelic" effect, I
suppose! The distance between the two tapers' vantage points makes for kind of a "surround
sound" effect!

This recording suffered from a problem common to audience recordings of this vintage - most of
the signal is dominated by bass frequencies with barely-audible midrange and highs. It sounded
like Jimi and his band were playing at the end of a very long tunnel! I did my best to fix the
problem without emphasizing in the process the rather ugly distortion that this issue often masks.
I settled upon a compromise EQ setting which allows you to hear Jimi's voice and guitar a lot
more distinctly than before while minimizing sonic nasties. There was noticeably more analogue
tape hiss in the left channel than the right. I've reduced it; I felt midrange and high frequencies
would suffer if I tried to completely eliminate it. The right channel has soft clicks throughout.
This is an issue which I've seen on low-fi tape decks and recordings made on them. Adobe
Audition's de-clicker got rid of them but it also acted upon other parts of the signal, so I elected
not to use it. It's really no worse than listening to a vinyl record that's in good condition but has been enjoyed, on good equipment, just a few times too many. Given that you cannot make a low-
fi recording hi-fi, I've done my best to make this historic recording more listenable and
enjoyable. You may judge for yourself whether my version is an improvement over the previous
one by checking out the MP3 samples below. In any case, enjoy!
