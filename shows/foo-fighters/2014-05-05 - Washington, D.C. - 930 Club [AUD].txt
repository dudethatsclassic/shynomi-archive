MISC NOTES:
This show was billed as "Big Tony's 50th Birthday Celebration," and was slated to be a night of music featuring the legendary DC go-go musician Big Tony with his band Trouble Funk, along with several other notable DC-area punk / go-go musicians.  Dave Grohl was advertised as the "host" of the festivities.  930 Club was very strict with their ticketing for the event, opting not to sell any tickets online, and instead requiring that customers buy tickets in-person through the venue's box office.  Each customer was allowed no more than two tickets, upon each of which was printed with the purchaser's name.  Purchasers were advised that the bearer of the second ticket would be required to enter the venue with the purchaser, at which point ID would be checked against the name on the tickets.  I felt the whole ticket buying process was a little extreme for a simple birthday celebration, but I dutifully waited in line the first morning that they went on sale and bought a single ticket for $37.50, expecting a really neat mash-up of all types of musicians playing and jamming together to some fine go-go rythmes.

As soon as I arrived to 930 on the day of the show I noticed several large production tractor-trailers for touring equipment and camera crews.  The line to enter was considerably longer than it would normally be for a show.  Rumors abounded of a Foo Fighters show after the advertised festivities and as this tape would indicate, the rumors proved true!  Doors were shortly after 7pm and the show started at 8pm with an opening set from "The Don't Need Its," featuring Darryl Jenifer on bass (of "Bad Brains"), Dr. Know on guitar (of "Bad Brains"), Pete Stahl on vocals (of "Scream," a band Dave Grohl played with in the mid / late 80's), and Dave Grohl on drums.  After "The Don't Need Its" played an approximately 45min set, "Trouble Funk" took the stage with their enigmatic front man, Big Tony for a nearly 90-minute set of go-go.  Dave Grohl took the stage at shortly past 11pm, and started playing by himself before being joined on stage by the rest of the Foo Fighters and proceeding to play a 2 hour set.  The crowd went absolutely nuts and it will remain a legendary night of fantastic music for those 1,200 music fans lucky enough to be in attendance.  I recorded all three acts.

The first track on this recording ("00 - Dave Grohl Show Intro") is a 3min intro Dave Grohl gave to the crowd.  He took the stage and gave this introduction about 15 minutes before the first band came on stage.  This introductory track is included as track 00 on all three of recordings I made at this show.
