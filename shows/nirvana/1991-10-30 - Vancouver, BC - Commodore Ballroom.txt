TRANSFER/EDITING NOTES:

I haven't really been doing a whole lot of work on bootleg recordings for a while as I've been busy writing and recording my own music but this is a performance that I had been trying to locate for some time and here it is! Being from Canada and being a Nirvana fan, I am always interested in recordings of shows they did up north. I recall first hearing this performance in a vinyl shop in Vancouver. When I asked the clerk what show it was, he said it was from the Commodore performance in 1991. I asked him if I could get a copy but he said the guy who recorded the show and gave him a copy told him he could play it in the store but not circulate it. I was very intrigued and wondered if a copy had leaked out somewhere. This is such a copy! I had an inferior recording previously that must've been from a higher generation because it definitely didn't sound as good. 
Unfortunately the source of this recording was plagued by a constant ticking/clicking sound in the right channel, mostly audible during quiet sections as is stated in the source text file. I therefore made an effort to remove the bulk of these anomalies as well as do some light eqalizing and volume/channel balancing. Don't worry, no compression or limiting has been used. I removed as many clicks as I could individually using the Adobe Audition heal feature. There are some quiet sections however where there were way too many clicks/ticks to remove individually so in those cases I used the automatic click repair function of Adobe Audition. This is the only real noise reduction that has been used and it has little to no affect on the overall sound as very little is happening in the quiet sections where I used this feature.
It's clear that the sound improves somewhat as the show goes on as the sound guy was doing some drastic mixing changes early on. I have attempt to balance the levels during these moments as accurately as possible but the imbalance in the instrument mix is inherent in the original recording and thus can only be remedied to a certain degree. I have included a couple of samples to compare with the source recording as well. I hope this finds a home in the hands of Nirvana fans and is considered a welcome addition to the bootleg catalog. Enjoy!
