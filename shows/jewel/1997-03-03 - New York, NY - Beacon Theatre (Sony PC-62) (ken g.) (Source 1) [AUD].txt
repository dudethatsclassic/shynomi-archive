Much thanks goes out to Ken G. for capturing this night.
This was near the beginning of Jewel's "Tiny Lights' Tour, which i was fortunate to see a couple weeks after this show.
From the opening vocal notes of "Near You Always" to the ending of her signature Yodeling "Chime Bells", 
the crowd screams are sprinkled all through this concert.  20 seconds don't go by without the fans losing their minds.
Most of them getting to hear Jewel for the first time was an over whelming experience.  The haunting style of "Enter From The East"
 is one of my all-time favorites of Jewel's to date.  Jewel had the ability to draw out emotion and thought with her story-like song 
writing derived from her poetry as a young person.The likes which had not yet been seen in the 90's generation of new singer song-writers.
unfortunately her potency has long faded into a whimsical genre of either the country or pop category.

This in my opinion, by the end of 1997 tour marked an end of the modest and introspective Jewel which her fans originally were 
drawn too. Where each performance showed a level of determination that Jewel tried to prove herself to the crowd and the world.
This is a look into that period.  Once this was established Jewel backed off from her deep and thought provoking story writing 
Such as "Painters" and "Adrian" and went into a much safer direction.  Jewel lost much of her original fan base, 
and as being a member of the Jewel Digest,  I watched and read the fans dismay for her change in writing and performance style.Though her next album in 1999 did draw in her new spiritual fans, while still keeping some of her older fans appeased. Getting to witness Jewel in a theater or club setting is where you want to be. 
Arenas and amphitheaters have no intimacy, which was proven during her World 1999 tour.

For the repost,  I updated the source info and titled the tracks.  No changes to the flac's.

This isn't the best recording only because the crowd is over bearing at times.  
Even Jewel expresses her embarrassment from all the over zealous fans.
If you're a fan of the old Jewel and don't have this night in your collection, then you'll want this one.

The last track is from when Jewel joined the openers in performing a duet of Old Lovers House (w/Steve Poltz)
