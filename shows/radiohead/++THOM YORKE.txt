Review:
Since the tour for The King of Limbs finished in Melbourne way back in November 2012, Thom Yorke
has been a very busy musician - a Radiohead record, two solo albums, a few film soundtracks, three
albums with an entirely new band, and hundreds of shows. But not a single one of those shows was in
Australia, which is why the announcement earlier this year of the "Everything Tour" came as such a
big deal. Not only was he returning to tour the Asia-Pacific region, but he was playing Radiohead
songs solo, something he's been somewhat averse to doing throughout his career (at least in
comparison to what you might think). The two nights booked at the Sidney Myer Music Bowl (a large
outdoor amphitheatre that I was surprised the main band had never played at) were very hotly
anticipated, and I was glad to get tickets to the first of them.

The show turned out to not quite be what I'd anticipated - in particular, I wasn't expecting as
much of his glitch-influenced electronic music throughout the set as we got. But despite the odd
conceit of switching between acoustic versions of songs from the 90s and bassy computer songs from
the 10s, it worked surprisingly well and the setlist flowed. The solo songs were well chosen, with
more than half of The Eraser appearing (including the title track, a piano take that opened up the
set) and a few soundtrack choices - I wasn't much for the nightmare soundscape of 'Volk', but deep
cut 'Hearing Damage' (from the Twilight soundtrack, new for this tour) went down well. He even
played us a brand new song, 'Back in the Game', which was debuted in New Zealand earlier this week
and is a propulsive techno thumper.

But of course, the loudest cheers (apart from his cursing out of an audience member making a break
for it at the start of 'Truth Ray') were for the Radiohead songs, and he delivered ten, from seven
different albums, across the course of the night. Getting 'Let Down' almost right away was enough
to send me into orbit, so I was willing to cut him a lot of slack for the rest of the night, but
the choices were pretty good - 'Fake Plastic Trees' got big cheers from many, 'Present Tense' was
lovely, and the electronic reworks of 'All I Need' and 'Packt Like Sardines...' (a song that
Radiohead, bafflingly, have played live just once since 2001) bridged the gap between his two modes
stunningly.

This is a good but not great tape - there are some issues with wind noise and clicks on 'The
Eraser', and to a lesser extent on 'Last I Heard', because I was quite twitchy about making sure I
had all of my levels right for the recording. I cleaned up the former a little bit by turning one
35-second patch to mono (it was worse in the left channel), but it still sounds pretty bad and I'm
sorry about that. The other major change was boosting the quieter songs in the set up to peaks of
about -5dB, which still offers some difference from the pounding techno tracks but should be easier
to listen to. Regardless of all that, it was an absolutely wonderful night and I was blessed for
it.
