Tape appears to be low gen. Shot from floor, not far from / to right of, center stage. Very good capture of Courtney and Melissa throughout, with occasional shots of Eric, and a few shots of Patty on drums. Brilliant bright colors everywhere. The sound is a bit rough, with too much echo on drums at times. It all gets better as it goes though. The hand-held camera-work from the pit no less, is pretty steady for the most part. A work well done. 

Courtney is in fine voice. As she is wont to do she adds extra brilliant new bits to just about every song.  

Here she is on a stage where her beloved deceased only grew gigantic in stature on the way to conquering the stage and the world, becoming arguably the biggest star in the solar system. 

Court is feeling Kurt. In between the emotionally charged songs and performances, she gets distracted. (Imagine that.) At the same time Courtney talks back to the crowd engaging her. She gives doubly back to the music. 

She plays PennyRoyal Tea solo. She asks Eric to join in. Instead the crowd does. Finally Eric jumps on board. At song end Courtney points heavenward.

Giant ventilating fans offstage are tossing her hair, dress and spirit around. She is getting so blown away in song.


** Biggest flaw [and only just now discovered] **  

The Reading set-closer Time To Kill ...is edited! 

On the radio version, that is. 

When the Reading highlights are later aired, edited out are curse words, time and magic.

Oops! They only left out the highest highlight of the entire festival.

"No!" 

So anyway, near the end of the still unreleased classic Courtney song, Time To Kill... Per usual, pressed up against a wall of bass rhythm rallying against a hail of guitar feedback, Courtney screams repeatedly, "No!" Over and over for about 30 seconds.

On the radio. But on the video, the full show, she howls repeatedly "No!" But for more than ever before, for about 1:30. 

And the missing part from the radio-audio -- the middle section -- contains one of the happening live moments in the history of Courtney and Hole and both rock and roll -- Her Howls Of Abandon -- the most intense, overwhelming and moving set of screams of shock and realization ever by her. Which considering it's Courtney, that means likely by anyone ever.


When watching the video through to the end for the first time today, listening/seeing/absorbing finally that full version of that song... 

For the first time I was so moved I felt like I was gone back there with her as it suddenly seems to me she is reliving the very moment when a little over a year ago, she first found out that Kurt was dead, and much more than that he had killed himself. 


And then when all is said and sung and gone and done, Courtney smashes up the stage Kurt-style. She even tackles and wrestles a big drum and stand to the floor. 

That is the last sight of her on screen, as Eric finishes fedding back and the scene fades to black.
