This is a significant recording since it is one of the very few concerts we have from 1966.
Richard Davis is replacing Ron Carter. The show has been seeded already on Dime a number 
of times, one version in remarkably good sound. The present version comes from a possibly
earlier-generation tape, and after remastering, the sound is as good as it gets. Enjoy.


Notes on the remastering
========================

I had three sources to choose from:
1. The version seeded several times on Dime, first upped by jazzcdburner in 2005 (#31895) and
   pitch-corrected by flambay (#326580). Remarkably good sound (A/A-)
2. A version seeded also on Dime (#605497), ripped from the Bootleg CD Sunburn 9339880. 
   Much poorer sound (B)
3. A version I received on CD-R in a trade in the early 2000s. Soundwise indistinguishable
   from the first version to my ears, but it seemed to have a bit more dynamic range. Clearly
   a different equalization when viewed with an audio editor (small differences, though).
   Plus, it had "My funny Valentine" in the right place, as conjectured on Peter Losin's site,
   with a seamless transition to "All Blues". So possibly a bit earlier in the chain than the 
   first version. 

I chose this last version for the remastering. One goal was to retain the remarkable clarity 
with which Tony Williams' cymbals were recorded.

- Adjusted the pitch trackwise to the version seeded by flambay, so this should be hopefully correct.
- Removed a lot of clicks and glitches.
- With Har-Bal, I changed the overall equalization so that it resembles the Plugged Nickel recordings
  from a year earlier. Slightly diminished the bass drum, which was too dominant. 
- Used Izotope Ozone 11 Advanced and Neutron 10 and let them do their magic with respect to clarity, impact, stabilization.
- Made a stem separation with Acoustica to rebalance the instruments. Piano and bass were too soft, drums too loud.
- Used the stem separation to pan the instruments, moved the piano a bit to the left, drums a bit 
  to the right, generating a stereo effect that sounds natural to my ears.
- Diminished the noise floor by 3 dB.
- Repaired Miles' frequent overblowing of the microphone with RX 10's De-plosive module.

"So what" is a thing in itself. Tony Williams hits his drums so hard you can barely hear the other instruments.
I had to attenuate the drums by a full 6 dB to create a listenable version. I have included the unattenuated
version (So what.alternative) but "commented it out". If you want to listen to it, rename the file 
extension from flac_ to flac.
