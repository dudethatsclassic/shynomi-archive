This tour was Matthew Sweet's first since before the pandemic — he'd initially stayed off the road because of health concerns — and the rust showed a bit, in both some faltering vocals in places and some poorly calibrated stage banter, too long in some places and entirely absent during long tuning breaks. Add in a sound engineer who needed a few songs to dial in the levels, and a Friday night crowd seemingly determined to talk over anything happening on stage, and conditions weren't ideal here.

But! This is still a great set of some of Sweet's best songs, and he was clearly relishing getting back in front of audiences with an excellent band, including for the first time Debbi Peterson of the Bangles on drums. And my recording location in the side balcony ended up working well to pick up clear sound from the PA and keep away from the talkers downstairs. I'm still hoping a recording turns up from earlier in the tour (I've heard excellent things about the Chicago show), but this is still a fun time.

If you've been out of touch with Matthew Sweet since the '90s, he has a ton of good recent albums, the last few of which are available at https://www.theconnextion.com/matthewsweet/matthewsweet_index.cfm?CatID=899&prodlist=1. (I especially recommend "Tomorrow Forever" — the song "Come Correct" is as solid an earworm as any of his classics.) And he's apparently working on a new album called "Midsommar," as he explains at length on Track 6, so we have both that and hopefully a subsequent tour to look forward to.
