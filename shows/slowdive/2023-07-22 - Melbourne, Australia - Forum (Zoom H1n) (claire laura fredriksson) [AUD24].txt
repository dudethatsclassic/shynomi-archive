Review:
Slowdive, one of the defining bands of the shoegaze scene, have made their way into the 2020s with
an adoring fanbase. Set to tour Australia for the third time in April 2022, performing some shows
with the Daydream Festival and others at headlining bills, the band had to pull out literally the
day before the first show due to a back injury to drummer Simon Scott. Daydream went off without a
hitch (see my tapes of that event), Slowdive quickly announced a rescheduled Australian tour - with
a new headlining date in Melbourne as a makeup - and three months and $90 later I was ready to see
them.

I had high expectations for the band, but low expectations for the crowd - they've been quite big
with the TikTok set, and the length of the line up Hosier Lane (all people my age, which makes me
feel like a raging hypocrite but oh well) didn't thrill me. As it turns out, most of the audience
was chill; the exception was an overly loud bonehead a few metres backwards and to the left of me.
Fortunately my mics aren't omnidirectional, unlike my ears, so you don't get to hear just how
annoying he was - apart from the crowd, I think it's a pretty good recording, although my ears are
shot as I write this so take it with a grain of salt.

The expectations were met, though - this was an excellent performance, everything you could wish
for from a band whose return would've seemed a huge surprise thirty years ago. While they've
physically changed in that time (Rachel was sporting a split-dyed hairdo that wouldn't look too out
of place on Siouxsie Sioux), the musical elements are just as awe-inspiring as they would have been
at the time. As wise of a move as it was to put the three songs the crowd most wanted in sequence
(and you can definitely hear that reaction), I fell in love with the more abstract and sprawling
songs like 'Star Roving'; they sounded incredible in the big open air. Behind them they had a
series of psychedelic visualisations waving around for every song, which were also quite nice to
look at (and easier to see than the band). Possibly best of all was the Syd Barrett cover that
closed the main set, minutes of ambient post-rock wash steadily building to an extraordinary
crescendo - I was surprised that they came out for an encore, I couldn't have thought anything
could follow that up. Still an outstanding band, best of luck to anyone who's trying to see them on
their upcoming tour.
